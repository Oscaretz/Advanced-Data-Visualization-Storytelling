# Who, What, How — Relational Data Visualization

**Team:** Moises’ Team  
**Project:** Portfolio – CheckPoint 3 (Relational Data Visualization)  
**Members:** Luis Arturo Michel Pérez, Oscar Martínez Estévez, Moisés Jesús Carrillo Alonso, Braulio Jesús Pérez Tamayo, Gerardo Hernández Widman, Mario David Hernández Pantoja  

---

## WHO — Target Audiences

| Audience | Role in Decision-Making | Information Needs | Visualization Focus |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Executives** | Approve strategic decisions and allocate resources. | High-level insights about which movies or genres drive network influence and audience reach. | Centrality distributions and summary dashboards that highlight key influencers. |
| **Marketing Team** | Design and target campaigns. | Identify movies that serve as bridges between audience segments and genres. | Correlation heatmaps and scatter plots linking centrality to ratings and popularity. |
| **Content Creators** | Develop new content aligned with audience interests. | Understand what makes a movie central or influential in the network structure. | Top movies bar charts and scatter plots showing how creative choices affect reach. |
| **Investors** | Evaluate potential for growth and long-term audience retention. | Assess relationships between influence, popularity, and stability in the network. | Aggregated metrics, trends, and centrality-based impact visualizations. |

---

## WHAT — Desired Actions or Decisions

| Visualization | Primary Decision or Action | Use Case |
|----------------|----------------------------|-----------|
| **Distribution of Centrality Metrics** | Recognize whether influence is concentrated or distributed evenly across movies. | Guides strategic decisions about diversifying promotion or focusing on key titles. |
| **Correlation Heatmap** | Identify which factors (popularity, rating) align with network centrality. | Supports data-driven marketing and production priorities. |
| **Grouped Bar Chart (Top Movies)** | Decide which movies deserve the highest promotional investment based on multi-metric performance. | Optimizes resource allocation to titles with proven network impact. |
| **Scatter Plots (Rating vs Centrality)** | Understand how structural influence relates to real-world audience success. | Encourages cross-functional collaboration between data teams and creative departments. |

---

## HOW — Data as Supporting Evidence

* The data visualizations **support decisions** rather than serve as the focus of analysis.  
* Each chart functions as a **communication tool** that translates complex network data into actionable insights.  
* **Executives and investors** can use summarized visuals to make quick strategic judgments without technical interpretation.  
* **Marketing and content teams** rely on relational evidence to refine targeting, storytelling, and genre blending strategies.  
* The overall narrative connects **quantitative indicators (centrality, popularity, rating)** with **qualitative outcomes (influence, audience engagement)** to promote data-informed creativity.

---

## Summary

This framework ensures that:

* Each visualization has a **specific audience** and **clear purpose**.  
* The **intended action** is explicit and measurable.  
* The **data** is used to **support storytelling and decision-making**, rather than overwhelm stakeholders with technical detail.

The result is a set of visualizations that bridge analytical insights and strategic decisions, empowering all stakeholders to understand and act upon the relationships within the movie network.
